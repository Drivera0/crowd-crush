Pulse

Early warning for crowd crushes, using the phones already in the crowd.

Dan Rivera

The problem

159

lives lost in the Itaewon crowd crush, Seoul, 29 October 2022. 158 people were crushed in one narrow alley.

Nobody inside a crush can see it building. Nobody outside can either.

The insight

A crush is a push that travels from person to person.

Dancing: everyone at once

Same motion, same moment. Ignored.

A push: one after another

Same motion, a fraction of a second later, person to person.

How it works

Math detects. AI explains.

Phones

A web page from one QR code. Ten motion readings a second.

One Go server

Clock sync, neighbour comparison every 250 ms, density, position estimate.

People

Staff: one sentence and one action, read aloud. Sign and zone lights. Each phone: which way to move.

Gemini writes the sentence and reads floor plans. ElevenLabs speaks it. If either is down, the alarm still fires with a template sentence.

In the crowd

Crowd danger near you

Move diagonally, not against the push.

Move this way.

The steward gets one sentence and one action. The person in the crowd gets a direction: sideways out of a push, toward space, toward an open exit.

What breaks

You can't see a 2-metre knot through 5 metres of GPS error.

Pushes caught, realistic GPS 80 / 80

Packing caught red, realistic GPS 0 / 80 (59 yellow)

Surges caught with the position estimator 58 / 60, ~5 s late

Packing needs positions good to about a metre: tap your spot, seats or sections, or Bluetooth ranging in a native app.

Built to run an event

One Go server. 1,000 phones. Nothing dropped.

Console

Setup checklist, watch areas with rules, alert cards with Acknowledge and Resolve, alert drills, escalation.

Resilient

Phones relay for each other over WebRTC. Sign and lights run over USB, no Wi-Fi. Every outside service falls back.

Private

A random ID, venue metres and motion numbers. No names, contacts, audio or photos. Raw GPS never stored.

Load test: 1,000 fake phones at 10 readings a second, about 10,000 messages a second, dashboard steady at 10 Hz (loadtest.md).

Built with

What each sponsor does

Tiger Data

Every motion reading and alert in TimescaleDB hypertables, batched with COPY; a per-zone, per-second continuous aggregate; recorded runs replay from it.

Google Gemini

Writes each alert as one headline and one action, as structured JSON, and reads uploaded floor plans. Never decides what is dangerous; 5 s timeout, then a template.

ElevenLabs

Speaks every briefing aloud for stewards who are not watching the screen, and re-voices alerts nobody acknowledged.

.Tech domain

The short HTTPS address behind the join QR code. Phones need HTTPS for their motion sensors.

Every service can be down or missing its key: Pulse still detects and alerts, with local files, template sentences and the browser's voice.
